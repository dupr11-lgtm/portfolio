## Starting point

A group project in the Ouro valley, in Porto. The valley is right there, visible from the high ground, but cut off from the neighbourhood by the steep terrain and the cars.
My part of the scheme sits on a cliff face, reached from a neighbourhood higher up. It takes the form of an intergenerational toy and games library, set into a system of terraces designed to make the slope walkable.
The aim was not to build an object but to make a way into the park: a threshold between the upper town and the valley.

## Walk-through

Made with Archicad, Midjourney and Photoshop.
Start with what you see in the Ouro neighbourhood: the valley is passed through, never lived in.
Take the leftover strips now occupied by cars as raw material.
Turn them into ‘gates’: open thresholds that lead along planted routes and slow people down.
Compose atmospheres rather than segments: pauses, encounters, lingering views.
The games library fits into this system: two volumes on the cliff, split by a central passage.
The passage is a true threshold: shelter, resting place, viewpoint, then the way down to the Douro.
Stage the reveal: the bridge, the river and the valley unfold like a performance.
No stand-alone object. Just a way through.

## Critical reflection

The terracing makes the slope accessible, but it also erases it. Rather than following the topography, we correct it. The descent becomes a managed route, where it could have been something more physical.
The games library adds focal points to the group scheme that draw people towards the valley, but it cannot bring the valley to life on its own. It relies on someone deciding to make the descent, and that dependence weakens the whole.
What the project lacks is a strong focal point at the top to tie everything together. We could have started from the slope and made it the organising constraint, instead of fighting it.
