## Starting point

A studio on individual practice. The starting point: the Nakagin Capsule Tower, a piece of modular architecture built on the repetition of identical capsules.
Each capsule looks the same as the next, and becomes something to make your own.
I chose to take over one capsule as a private place: a photographic darkroom. It is an enclosed, controlled space, given over entirely to light.
My work happens inside the cell: I modulate the light, create an atmosphere and play with time, the time spent waiting for the print.
The aim: to turn a standard cell into a singular, almost secret experience.

## Walk-through

Start from a neutral form: an empty capsule, the same for everyone.
The Nakagin Tower: module, repetition, standardisation.
Inside, I go looking for difference.
Turn the capsule into a darkroom: enclosed, almost secret.
Work with light as a material: it comes in, changes, becomes an image.
Build a slow atmosphere: waiting for the print, the smell of chemicals, silence, concentration.
The space does more than house an activity: it acts on perception.
Put together, the capsules form one structure, and yet every experience is different.

## Critical reflection

The capsule imposes a tiny, repetitive volume. Here, that constraint becomes a resource.
By bringing in photographic development, I move from a fit-out to an apparatus: light is no longer just illumination, it produces the image. That is a clean break from the technical projects.
But the project turns in on itself. It is a closed, coherent world that has little to say to the outside, to the other capsules or to the tower. Its strength is also its withdrawal.
