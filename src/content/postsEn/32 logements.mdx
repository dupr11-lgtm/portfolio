## Starting point

Paris, 14th arrondissement. A courtyard hemmed in by towers, almost invisible from the street.
I began with a simple question: how do you bring light and privacy into so constrained a site?
I chose to split the programme into two separate volumes, one for students and one for families.
The garden became the real heart of the project: not a leftover space, but the only link between the street and the back of the plot.
It was my first time working on housing in a dense setting. It taught me to read a site as a sequence rather than a plan.

## Walk-through

Made with Archicad.
Two buildings tucked away in a courtyard. From the street to the garden, the world changes.
The first building lines up with the street. It houses students, in studio flats and shared spaces.
A passage opens towards the heart of the plot. The noise and the crowds fade. The ground turns green. The eye is released from the façades.
At the back, the second building appears, set back and lower. Attached to the existing wall, it comes down to meet the garden. It holds larger flats for long-term residents.
People reach their front doors along access decks screened by cladding. This filter adds depth: you feel sheltered, close to your door without being exposed.
On the ground floor, every flat has its own small garden. This planted cul-de-sac cuts down on passers-by and gives you the feeling of being at home, set apart.
Bit by bit, the atmosphere changes: from open street to quiet garden, from half-sheltered walkway to the home.
The project proposes thresholds rather than a hard division: privacy is earned gradually, despite the towers all around.

## Critical reflection

The project works by accumulating thresholds. Each stage (street, garden, access deck, threshold) puts some distance between you and the city. It is sound and coherent.
But that logic remains implicit. I did not set it out as a principle from the start: it emerged bit by bit as the project went on.
The arrival sequence could have shaped everything else. Instead it merely goes along with it, because the principle came so late in the project.
