## Project starting point

Paris, 14th arrondissement. A courtyard enclosed between towers, almost invisible from the street.
I started from a simple question: how to bring light and privacy into such a constrained site?
I chose to split the programme into two distinct volumes: one for students, one for families.
The garden became the real heart of the project: not a residual space, but the only link between the street and the back of the plot.
It was the first time I worked on housing in a dense urban context. Here I learned to read a site not as a plan, but as a sequence.

## Crossing

Archicad used.

Two buildings hidden in a courtyard. From the street to the garden, the world changes.
A first building aligns with the street. It houses student flats, made of studios and shared spaces.
A passage opens towards the heart of the plot. The noise and the crowd fade away. The ground turns green. The eye frees itself from the façades.
At the back, the second building appears set back, lower. Attached to the existing wall, it touches the garden. It hosts larger apartments for stable residents.
Circulation runs along exterior galleries, protected by cladding. This filter creates depth: you feel sheltered, close to your entrance without being exposed.
On the ground floor, each apartment has a mini-garden. This planted cul-de-sac limits through traffic and gives the feeling of being at home, away from it all.
Step by step, the atmospheres shift: from the open street to the peaceful garden, from the semi-sheltered corridor to the domestic space.
The project proposes thresholds rather than a sharp separation: privacy is gained progressively, despite the towers all around.

### Critical review

The project works through an accumulation of thresholds. Each step, street, garden, gallery, threshold, establishes a distance from the city. It is accurate and coherent.
But this logic stays implicit. I did not set it as a principle from the start: it came in successive touches, as the project developed.
The arrival sequence could have structured everything else. It only accompanies the rest, because that principle arrived late in the project.
