## Starting point

Châtellerault, Place Émile Zola. A former car park that has become nothing more than a through route.
I crossed this square every day without ever stopping. That was where the project came from: a space you pass through but never stay in.
The aim was to give it some substance again, through the ground rather than a grand gesture. Planting becomes a way of organising the space, creating shade, places to pause and loose, informal routes.

## Walk-through

Place Émile Zola, Châtellerault: always crossed, hardly ever lived in.
A former car park turned into a mere through route.
Bring everyday life back to the space.
Planting as something to use: it guides, calms and gives shade.
Around the trees, pockets of rest: places to stop, not just pass.
The ground becomes a device: permeable, absorbent, less uniform.
Wood-chip paths and planted areas trace loose, porous routes.
The square changes its rhythm, not just its form.

## Critical reflection

The strategy is sound: bringing in planting to make up for the lack of comfort and places to rest. It answers the analysis of the site.
But the solution is predictable. Planting trees where there are none is logical, not transformative.
The square will be a nicer place to walk through. Will it become somewhere people stay? That is far from certain. The project improves the crossing without guaranteeing anyone will use the place.
