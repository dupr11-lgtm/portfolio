## Project starting point

Châtellerault, Place Émile Zola. A former car park that became a simple thoroughfare.
I crossed this square every day without ever stopping. That observation gave birth to the project: a space one crosses, but does not inhabit.
The goal was to give it body again, not through a strong gesture, but through the ground. Vegetation becomes an organising tool: creating shade, pauses, flexible routes.

### Crossing

Place Émile Zola, Châtellerault: crossed constantly, almost never inhabited.
Former car park converted into a simple transit axis.
Giving this space back its everyday life.
Vegetation as a tool of use: it guides, calms, brings shade.
Around the trees, pockets of pause: places where you can stop, not just pass.
The ground becomes a device: permeable, infiltrating, less uniform.
Wood-chip paths, planted surfaces: tracing flexible, porous routes.
The square changes its rhythm, not just its form.

### Critical review

The strategy is right: introducing vegetation to correct the lack of comfort and the absence of resting areas. It answers the diagnosis.
But the solution stays predictable. Planting trees where there are none is logical, not transformative.
The square will be more pleasant to cross. Will it become a place where people stay? That is not certain. The project improves the crossing without guaranteeing the use.
