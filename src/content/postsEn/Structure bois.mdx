## Project starting point

Design exercise: a glued laminated timber frame on a 6 × 6 m grid, with imposed spans and an opening in the floor.
It was not an exercise in form, but in reading: understanding how vertical and horizontal forces circulate.

### Crossing

6×6 m grid.
Imposed spans and voids (floor opening, passages).
Comparing stabilisation systems: St. Andrew's crosses, portals, L-shaped forms.
Reading the mechanism: vertical forces (beams/joists/floor) vs horizontal ones (bracing).
Flitched beams: strengthening the span, supporting joists and floor. Floor opening: interrupting, then taking up again (trimming, redistribution).
Triangulate where it works. Free up where it is crossed.
Spans and voids leave their mark. Stability becomes visible.

## Critical review

The framework was very tight: the grid and the voids were imposed, the bracing systems given.
What works: the structure is readable, the forces are well taken up, the floor opening is handled correctly.
What is missing: a position taken. The answer almost seems obvious, which shifts the interest towards the precision of the model. You acquire tools, but you are not asking questions yet.
