## Starting point

A design exercise: a glulam frame on a 6 × 6 m grid, with set spans and a set floor opening.
It was an exercise in reading rather than form: understanding how vertical and horizontal forces travel through the structure.

## Walk-through

6 × 6 m grid.
Set spans and voids (floor opening, passages).
Compare ways of stabilising the frame: St Andrew’s crosses, portal frames, L-shaped frames.
Read the mechanism: vertical loads (beams, joists, floor) and horizontal loads (bracing).
Twin beams: extending the span, carrying the joists and floor. Floor opening: interrupt, then pick up again (trimmer, load redistribution).
Triangulate where the structure is working hard. Open up where people pass through.
Spans and voids leave their mark. Stability becomes visible.

## Critical reflection

The brief was very tight: the grid and voids were set, and the bracing systems were given.
What works: the structure is legible, the loads are properly carried and the floor opening is correctly handled.
What is missing: a point of view. The answer seems almost obvious, which shifts the interest onto the precision of the model. You pick up tools, but you are not yet asking questions.
