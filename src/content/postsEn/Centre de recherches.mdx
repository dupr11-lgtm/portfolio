## Project starting point

Montserrat, Soufrière Hills volcano. A site rendered partly uninhabitable by an eruption in 1997.
Choosing to build a research centre as close as possible to the volcano was not obvious. It is precisely this tension, observing from inside the danger, that interested me.
I built the project around a double state: an ordinary life at the surface, an underground refuge for alerts. It is neither a normal residence nor a classic bunker. It is a space made to live with instability.

## Crossing

Building with instability.
The Soufrière Hills volcano changed Montserrat forever.
Thinking of the centre as a superimposition of two realities: everyday life at the surface, a protected space in depth.
Bringing together research on wildlife, flora and climate, while keeping risk at the centre: ash, pyroclasts, lahars.
At the first sign of an alert, everything shifts.
The bunker becomes the main architecture: buried to filter the air and protect, but pierced with a skylight to capture light.
The refuge on two levels: rest and hygiene, then communal life and endurance.
The whole is self-sufficient: solar, batteries, filtered rainwater.
Not resisting the territory. Creating a way to stay on it.

## Critical review

The initial idea holds: building where the risk is greatest to observe it better. But the project does not fully commit to it.
The bunker quickly takes over. One speaks less of research than of survival. This shift is interesting, but not fully owned.
The skylight is the sign of it: keeping light under a rain of ash is both seductive and fragile. The project evokes an extreme scenario without embracing all its violence. The sensory experience is there, but at the cost of a distance from the outside.
