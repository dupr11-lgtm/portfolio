## Starting point

I have known Avenue Daumesnil for years, on foot and by bike. I use this artery often and see what is wrong with it every day: pedestrians and cyclists in conflict, junctions that are hard to read, a sense of danger at rush hour.
The project grew out of that daily frustration.
I wanted to rework the avenue by layering three logics: movement, landscape and water management. The idea was not to start again from scratch but to give each use its own clear, continuous space.

## Walk-through

An artery: Avenue Daumesnil.
Linking Bastille to Vincennes, like an urban river.
Three layers: movement, landscape, water.
A cycle lane down the middle, lined with planting to organise the flows.
The ground becomes a filter: swales, permeable pavements, asphalt that breathes.
The water collected runs to the Bois de Vincennes, and any surplus on to the Marne.
Understated solar street lamps soften the night.
Not just crossing the avenue, but making it work as a proper landscape.

## Critical reflection

The project makes the avenue legible. It organises, clarifies and structures.
But it treats the avenue as one standard section, the same from Bastille to Vincennes. The junctions, where the conflicts are real and the flows cross, are left untouched. And yet every junction is different and needs careful attention.
That is the limit: I redrew the avenue from my own experience of passing along it, without checking whether it could actually take the traffic. The project remains a coherent vision, but an abstract one.
