## Project starting point

I have known Avenue Daumesnil on foot and by bike for a long time. It is an artery I cross often and whose dysfunctions I see every day: pedestrians and cyclists in conflict, crossings that are hard to read, a feeling of danger at peak hours.
This project was born from that daily frustration.
I wanted to recompose the avenue by layering three logics: mobility, landscape and water management. The idea was not to redo everything, but to give every use a clear and continuous space.

### Crossing

An artery: Avenue Daumesnil.
Linking Bastille to Vincennes, like an urban river.
Three layers: mobility, landscape, water.
A cycle lane in the middle, lined with planting to structure the flows.
The ground becomes a filter: swales, permeable pavements, asphalt that breathes.
Collected water flows to the Bois, then to the Marne when there is a surplus.
Sober street lamps, powered by the sun, soften the night.
Not just crossing the avenue, but making it work as a real landscape.

### Critical review

The project gives the avenue readability. It organises, it clarifies, it structures.
But it treats the avenue as a standard section, identical from Bastille to Vincennes. The intersections, where the conflicts are real and the flows cross, are not addressed. Yet every intersection is unique and deserves close attention.
That is the limit: I redrew the avenue from my own experience of passing through, without checking whether it would absorb the traffic volumes. The project remains a coherent but abstract vision.
