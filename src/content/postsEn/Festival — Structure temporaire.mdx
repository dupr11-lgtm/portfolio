## Project starting point

On the banks of the Seine. A ditch cuts off access to the landscape.
The idea was to turn this void into a path, not by filling it, but by crossing it at height.
I designed a light structure: standard scaffolding and recovered textile. This choice met the technical constraints of an ephemeral festival, but also the very idea of the project. The textile becomes a filter: first opaque, it holds the gaze. Then it tears, gradually. The Seine appears at the end.
This project does not build a space. It builds a duration. It holds the gaze.

## Crossing

On the banks of the Seine, a ditch blocks our way.
Turning this void into a path.
Dismantlable scaffolding, recovered textiles.
The simple frame, the light filtering skin.
The visitor climbs gently. Each step is a sequence.
At first, the fabric captures the view. Then it loosens.
The landscape arrives gradually.
Little by little, the Seine appears.
No immediate visual shock. We build the anticipation.

## Critical review

The project rests on a single principle: the ascent. It works in discovery, in the moment.
That is its strength and its limit. It assumes that people climb and, once at the top, the tension drops: there is nothing left to inhabit. The structure serves the experience, but never becomes a place in itself.
It is a deliberate choice. But it means the project only exists in movement. When still, it fades away.
