## Starting point

The banks of the Seine. A ditch cuts off access to the landscape.
The idea was to turn this void into a path, by crossing it at height rather than filling it in.
I designed a lightweight structure of standard scaffolding and salvaged textile. The choice met the technical constraints of a short-lived festival, but it also came from the idea itself. The textile acts as a filter: opaque at first, it holds your gaze. Then, by degrees, it tears. The Seine appears at the very end.
This project does not build a space. It builds a length of time. It holds your gaze.

## Walk-through

On the banks of the Seine, a ditch bars the way.
Turning this void into a path.
Demountable scaffolding, salvaged textiles.
A simple frame, a light, filtering skin.
Visitors climb gently. Every step is a sequence.
At first, the fabric takes up the view. Then it slackens.
The landscape arrives by degrees.
Little by little, the Seine appears.
No sudden visual shock. The anticipation is what gets built.

## Critical reflection

The project rests on one principle: the climb. It works through discovery, in the moment.
That is both its strength and its limit. It assumes people will climb, and once they reach the top the tension falls away: there is nothing left to inhabit. The structure serves the experience but never becomes a place in its own right.
It was a deliberate choice. But it means the project only exists in motion. Once you stop, it fades.
