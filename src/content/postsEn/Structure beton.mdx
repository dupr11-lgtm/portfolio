## Starting point

An exercise on load paths.
Two elements give the project its structure: a main concrete volume (continuity, inertia) and a triangulated truss roof.
The aim was to explain how the forces travel from the roof down to the foundations. The openings (windows, doors) become controlled cuts, with lintels, piers and sills placed exactly where they are needed.
The emphasis was on the mechanics of the structure rather than on formal effect.

## Walk-through

Concrete body: supports, continuity, inertia.
Roof: triangulated truss.
From roof to foundations, follow the load path.
Openings = controlled cuts: lintels, sills and piers exactly where they are needed.
The structure sets the position of the windows, not the other way round.
Less decoration, more visible mechanics.

## Critical reflection

As with the timber structure, the brief was very strict.
The project is coherent: you can see at a glance how the loads come down and where they are carried. But that coherence comes from the brief, not from any stance of my own.
It shows I grasped the principle. It does not take the thinking any further.
In the end, what counts most is the model: the precision of the formwork, the crispness of the finish. It is a good technical exercise, but not yet a personal vision.
