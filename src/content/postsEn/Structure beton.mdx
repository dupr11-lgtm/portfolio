## Project starting point

Exercise on load paths.
Two elements structure the project: a main concrete volume (continuity, inertia) and a roof made of triangulated trusses.
The goal was to explain the path of the forces from the roof to the foundations. The openings (windows, doors) become controlled cuts: lintels, piers and sills are placed where they should be.
The emphasis was on the mechanics of the structure, not on the formal effect.

## Crossing

Concrete body: supports, continuity, inertia.
Roof: triangulated truss.
From the roof to the foundations, following the path of the loads.
Openings = controlled cuts: lintels, sills, piers exactly where they should be.
Window placement dictated by the structure, not the other way around.
Less decoration, more visible mechanics.

## Critical review

As with the timber structure, the framework was very strict.
The project is coherent: at a glance you see how the loads descend and are taken up. But this coherence comes from the framework, not from a personal stance.
It shows that I understood the principle. It does not push the thinking further.
In the end, it is mainly the model that counts: the precision of the formwork, the clarity of the rendering. The project is a good technical exercise, but not yet a personal vision.
