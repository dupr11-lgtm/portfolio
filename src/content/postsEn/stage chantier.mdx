## On site

Clichy-la-Garenne. A wastewater pumping and pre-treatment works serving Paris.
My first contact with a real, live construction site, already shaped by its own constraints.
This hands-on placement, done in my first year, is on a civil engineering site where the works are being overhauled.
What I find is a place under constant pressure: safety rules, coordination between trades, non-stop traffic, difficult access.
As part of a team of formwork carpenters, I join in the daily work: putting up formwork, erecting falsework towers, passivating the reinforcement, preparing areas for pouring, scabbling the existing concrete so that the new will key in.
I also work on a very thick slab designed to carry heavy loads: setting the ducts, checking the reinforcement, pouring the concrete, vibrating it and testing its consistency.
The site sets the pace.
Open channels of sewage, noise, the unexpected, restricted access: nothing here is abstract.
Meanwhile, I watch how the site is organised more widely: programme meetings, coordinating the teams, dealing with setbacks, constant adjustments.

## What I learnt

The placement taught me rigour but, above all, adaptability.
Every task is part of a chain where you have to check, adjust and sometimes redo.
It showed me the gap between the drawing and the reality on the ground.
On paper, everything seems to hold together.
On site, everything gets negotiated, coordinated, corrected.
What I take from it: building means constantly juggling three things.
Adapting.
Coordinating.
Checking.
It is this reality of the site, as the place where the drawing gets translated, that now shapes the way I look at architecture.
