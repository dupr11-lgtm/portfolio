## Field immersion

Clichy-la-Garenne. Paris sewage pumping and pre-treatment plant.
First contact with a real, active construction site, already shaped by its own constraints.
This worker placement, carried out in the first year, takes place on a civil engineering site dedicated to the overhaul of the plant.
There I discover a place under permanent tension: safety rules, coordination between trades, continuous traffic, difficult access.
Integrated into a team of formworkers, I take part in the everyday gestures: setting up formwork, installing shoring towers, passivating the rebar, preparing pouring zones, roughening the existing concrete to ensure connections.
I also work on a thick slab designed to take heavy loads: laying conduits, monitoring the rebar, pouring the concrete, vibrating it, checking its consistency.
The site imposes its rhythm.
Open sewage, noise, surprises, access constraints: here, nothing is abstract.
At the same time, I observe its wider organisation: planning meetings, coordination of teams, management of unexpected events, constant adjustments.

## Lessons learned

This placement taught me rigour, but above all adaptation.
Each intervention is part of a chain where you have to check, adjust, sometimes redo.
I understood the gap between drawing and the ground.
On paper, everything seems held together.
On site, everything is negotiated, coordinated, corrected.
What I take away: building means constantly articulating three actions.
Adapting.
Coordinating.
Checking.
It is this reality of the construction site, as a place where drawing is translated, that now guides the way I look at architecture.
